## Frame

- Origin: the west end of curved feature **C1**'s chord, on the **room-side face**
  of the long wall. That is the first tick of the drawing's dimension chain and
  the only unambiguous datum on that wall.
- `+x` east along the long wall (the direction of travel from START); `+y` north,
  up out of the lower corridor toward the upper room; floor at `z = 0`.
- The long wall's room-side face **is** the line `y = 0`, so every dimension along
  it is directly an x-coordinate.
- Stored points are wall **faces**, because that is what a tape measure spans.
  Slabs extrude `thickness` away from the free space. The MuJoCo/MapV3 adapter is
  the single place that converts to the centreline convention those APIs use.

## Unresolved ambiguities

Nothing below is derivable from the labelled dimensions. Each is a named
parameter in `geometry.CHOSEN` and in the YAML's `underdetermined:` block;
changing one and re-running `scripts/build_demo_map.py` rebuilds everything.

1. **West stub — 1.50 m chosen** (sketch proportion ≈ 0.25 m). The dimension
   chain *starts* at C1, so the tape never measured how far the long and bottom
   walls run west of it. 1.50 m gives the robot a straight run to acquire the
   wall before the first curved feature (spawn needs 0.60 m clear; touch pitch is
   0.35 m). This is the largest licence taken, and it is route-relevant.
2. **Upper room west extent — 5.50 m chosen** (sketch ≈ 5.5 m at the corridor's
   local scale). Not route-relevant: the route turns east on entering the room.
3. **Upper room depth — 4.00 m chosen.** The sketch supports anywhere from 3.4 m
   to 7.7 m depending on which labelled datum sets the local vertical scale. Not
   route-relevant; the value keeps the scene compact.
4. **East stub past the doorway — 0.70 m chosen** (sketch 0.61–0.80 m). Drawn but
   unlabelled.
5. **Wall thickness — 0.10 m.** Absent from the single-line sketch; taken from the
   project standard, which is also `MAZE_WALL_THICKNESS` in the shipped asset.
6. **Faces vs centrelines.** The drawing does not say which its lines are. Faces
   were chosen, since tape measurements span surfaces. If they were centrelines
   instead, every clear span shrinks by 0.10 m (e.g. the corridor becomes 2.31 m).
7. **The lower corridor's west end is left open**, exactly as drawn — no wall
   closes it. The START region sits at that open mouth.
8. **The doorway symbol.** The task text calls it a doorway/goal opening and it is
   built as a gap in the south wall. In the drawing the hatched symbol could as
   easily be furniture against the wall. The metric extent (146.3 cm offset,
   162.56 cm width) is identical either way; only whether the robot could pass
   *through* differs, which the route never does.
9. **`146.3 cm` is the one labelled value that is not a whole half-inch.** Ten of
   the eleven are exact imperial readings — 23.5, 11.5, 266, 29, 18.5, 89, 95, 64,
   415 and 64 inches. `146.3 cm` is 57.598 in; `146.05 cm` would be exactly 57.5 in.
   The drawing is followed as written, but if the tape was imperial this dimension
   is probably 146.05 cm and the doorway sits 2.5 mm further west. Worth one
   re-measurement in the real room; it changes nothing for the demo.

Two further notes that are not ambiguities but are worth stating: the region north
of the long wall (west of the corridor) is outside the mapped space and is not
built; and the two curved features are **protrusions into** the lower corridor, not
recesses — the arcs are drawn on the free-space side of the wall line, and the
desired route's dip into them agrees.

## Demo route

Ten segments, 26.65 m, `START (−1.30, −0.65) → GOAL (15.04, 9.48)`. Sparse and
geometric — the only arcs are the three places the geometry genuinely bends.

| id | kind | follows | hand | length (m) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R0 | approach | W1 | left | 0.96 |
| R1 | curve_follow | C1 | left | 1.46 |
| R2 | wall_follow | W2 | left | 5.96 |
| R3 | curve_follow | C2 | left | 1.97 |
| R4 | wall_follow | W3 | left | 1.82 |
| R5 | **transit** | — | — | 1.85 |
| R6 | wall_follow | W6 | right | 9.48 |
| R7 | corner_turn | W6→W8 | right | 0.88 |
| R8 | wall_follow | W8 | right | 1.46 |
| R9 | goal_approach | DOOR | — | 0.81 |

A followed lane is the **offset curve of the wall envelope** at 0.56 m, not a
straight line at fixed offset. It has to be: C2 stands 0.47 m proud, so a straight
0.56 m lane would pass 0.09 m from its apex. Offsetting the envelope instead makes
each bow exactly a circular arc of radius `R + 0.56` — closed form — and holds the
gap to every followed surface at a constant 0.56 m, so the press stance is the same
step-in the whole way.

## Phase 5 — demo readiness

`scripts/check_demo_map_readiness.py`: **17 checks passed, 0 failed.**

- **Collision-free route geometry** — min surface clearance 0.510 m over 26.65 m;
  segment ends coincide to 1e-9; every sampled point inside declared free space.
- **G1 body clearance** — the G1 was compiled *into* the reconstructed room and
  stood at 53 poses along the route (29 robot collision geoms × 14 room geoms,
  `mj_geomDistance`): **0 penetrations, closest approach 0.226 m**, between the
  left tactile pad and C2's cylinder at the entry to the C2 bow. The measured G1
  footprint radius is 0.369 m against a 0.510 m route minimum.
- **Reachable tactile walls** — all seven followed segments hold a 0.510–0.560 m
  lane gap; a press is a 0.18–0.26 m step in, inside the measured 0.30–0.38 m
  stance window and under the 0.43 m wrist cap.
- **Start/goal placement** — 0.650 m and 0.966 m clear (spawn needs 0.60 m), both
  inside free space, goal centred on the doorway on the room side.
- **`lane_graph.maps_v3.validate`** reports nothing. The map offers 9 followable
  lanes, 75.6 m total, longest 13.9 m, 215 touch increments.

Compatibility observations (not failures):

- Wall thickness 0.10 m matches `MAZE_WALL_THICKNESS` exactly.
- Wall height is 1.8 m against the shipped asset's 1.5 m default. `mj_assets`
  gained an optional per-obstacle `half_height` (default unchanged) so this scene
  can be taller without moving the shared default.
- 14 injected entities (12 walls, 2 cylinders) → 28 hand contact sensors. The
  V2/V3 backend rewrites the FSM wall map wholesale, so 12 walls is not a capacity
  limit.
- **Scene extent 18.1 × 14.5 m, farthest wall point 21.0 m from the map origin.**
  The existing V3 rooms are 7–9 m and the wall-following task's `arena_radius`
  default is 5.0 m. Wall placement bypasses that radius, but nothing in this stack
  has been driven this far from its origin — the most likely place for a surprise.
- **The followed hand switches** left→right at R5. A follower that pins one hand
  cannot execute this route end to end.
- C1/C2 stand 0.29 m and 0.47 m proud; the V3 bulge vocabulary assumes ≤ 0.15 m so
  that a straight lane stays smooth. Here the lane must bow.

## Not done, deliberately

No planner heuristics were added and no `WALL_FOLLOW` controller work was done.
The map is **not** registered in `lane_graph.scene3.PRIORS`, so the belief planner
does not yet know it; wiring that up (and running a physical episode) is separate
work. The route is a reference path, not a plan the executive consumes.

# Scan-derived map

An iPhone LiDAR scan of the building (`out/demo_map/8_10_2026.glb`, 78 401 faces,
25 × 43 m) turned out to contain the demo room as a small part of a much larger
floor. It is an **independent instrument**, so it can do two things the drawing
cannot: corroborate the dimensions the tape measured, and supply the ones it
never did.

```bash
# once, needs trimesh + the 14 MB mesh -> a small JSON of wall faces
tactile_controller/.venv/bin/python scripts/extract_scan_walls.py
# thereafter numpy only
PYTHONPATH=src python3 scripts/build_scan_map.py
PYTHONPATH=src python3 scripts/check_demo_map_readiness.py --map scan
MUJOCO_GL=egl PYTHONPATH=src python3 scripts/render_demo_map.py --map scan
```

## Registration

ICP of the tape reconstruction's own wall faces against the scan's wall band
(near-vertical faces sampled 0.2–1.9 m above the floor). The invented upper-room
walls are excluded from the fit so they cannot drag it.

| | |
|---|---|
| transform | `scan_xy = R(89.787°) · map_xy + (11.098, 3.069)` |
| residual | median **25.8 mm**, p90 97.8 mm, over 4 862 correspondences |

The map frame is the anchor — the route, the tests and the MuJoCo build already
speak it, so the scan moves into it rather than the reverse.

## What the scan says about the tape

| feature | tape | scan | difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| lower corridor clear width | 1.6256 | 1.6810 | **+55 mm** |
| branch corridor clear width | 2.4130 | 2.3956 | **−17 mm** |
| long wall, origin → branch mouth | 10.3505 | 10.3315 | **−19 mm** |
| bottom wall → branch corridor end | 10.5410 | 12.1797 | **+1 639 mm** |

Three of four agree to within 55 mm — about what a phone mesh is worth over
these distances, and strong corroboration of the reconstruction. The fourth does
not, and the disagreement is 30× larger than any other.

**Both curved features are in the scan, where the reconstruction predicted them.**
Profiling the scanned wall found protrusions at map x = 0.00–0.56 and 7.50–8.09
(predicted centres 0.298 and 7.722), 0.24 m and 0.40 m proud. Their *positions*
confirm the tape; their *depths* do not match it (tape: 0.2921 and 0.4699),
because a phone mesh smooths a 0.3 m bump. So the scan-derived map **keeps the
tape's chord+sagitta circles** and takes everything else from the scan.

## The one thing the drawing gets wrong

`1054.1 cm` is drawn spanning the bottom wall up to the upper room's south wall —
I re-checked the extension lines at pixel level and that reading is correct. It
puts that wall at map y = 8.9154. The scan puts the branch corridor's end at
**y = 10.52**, 1.64 m further north.

Everything else in the drawing agrees with the scan to ≤ 55 mm, so the drawing is
the outlier here, not the scan. If `1054.1 cm` had been measured from the *long
wall* rather than the bottom wall it would give y = 10.541 — 19 mm from what the
scan finds, right in line with the other errors. That is the likely explanation,
but it is a guess about how the tape was held; what is certain is that the datum
as drawn is 1.64 m short. **Ambiguity #9 in the list above is therefore resolved,
and a tenth is added: the drawing's 1054.1 cm datum is unreliable.**

## What the scan resolved

Four of the six "underdetermined" values were guesses. The scan replaces them
with measurements:

| was chosen | value I picked | what the scan shows |
|---|---|---|
| west stub 1.50 m | a stub | the hallway **continues ~23 m west** — it is a building corridor, not a room end |
| upper room west extent 5.50 m | a rectangle | there is no such room; the branch corridor runs 10.5 m and opens into a **5.5 × 3 m end room** |
| upper room depth 4.00 m | 4.00 m | the end room's north wall is at y = 13.51 |
| east stub 0.70 m | a stub | the end room's east wall is at x ≈ 15.68, with an alcove and a partition at x ≈ 14.55 |

## What is represented, and what is not

Three defects in the first build, all found by measuring rather than by eye:

- **Overlapping boards.** One physical wall drifts (0.10 m over 26 m) and its
  surface is textured, so the extractor returned it as several collinear lines a
  few cm apart — each built as its own slab, visibly overlapping. Measured: 14
  overlapping pairs, 0.134 m².

  `_dedupe` now runs three passes per wall line, each to a fixpoint: **merge**
  lines that are one surface (capped by `max_spread`, so a real 0.17 m step in a
  wall survives instead of being averaged away), **drop** duplicates that cover
  each other, and then enforce the **invariant** that no two boards sit within
  one wall thickness and overlap — repaired by merging, never dropping, so the
  pass cannot lose geometry. A feature standing *proud* of a wall is left alone:
  it is a different surface and must intersect the wall it grows from.

- **A 0.9 m stub deleted the corridor's entire south wall.** The duplicate rule
  measured overlap against the *shorter* of the two faces, so the 0.9 m jut
  sitting 0.17 m off the 12.74 m south wall covered more than half of *the stub*
  and vetoed the wall. Duplicates must cover **each other**, so the threshold is
  now a fraction of the longer face.

  Worth recording *why* nothing caught it: every check in place was a safety
  check — clearance, penetrations, free space — and all of them get **easier**
  when a wall disappears. `test_every_long_extracted_face_survives_into_the_map`
  now asserts completeness directly: no extracted face ≥ 1 m may be absent from
  the built map.
- **The curved features were extracting as walls too.** The flat front of C1 came
  out as a short line 0.157 m proud, and the merge then chained *through* it —
  bridging the long wall straight across C1's chord and deleting the gap the
  feature is supposed to leave. C1/C2 samples are now excluded from the wall-face
  pass, since they are already cylinders. As a bonus this trimmed the wall runs
  back off C2's chord, which is where the earlier 0.10 m wall/cylinder overlap
  came from.
- **Everything that is not a long straight wall was being discarded.** A wall
  face is a line with free space on one side; that model has nothing to say about
  a door leaf, a jamb, a column or a desk. Those surfaces now go through a second
  pass: unclaimed band points are clustered by connectivity and each cluster
  becomes its minimum-area oriented box **with its own z extent** — a desk is not
  1.8 m tall, and a window sill does not start at the floor. 17 boxes, ranging
  from 0.21 × 0.16 m jambs to a 1.15 × 0.77 m desk-height object in the end room.

After all three, **90.5 % of the scanned wall surface inside the extraction
window is claimed by a wall face**, and the rest is carried by the clutter boxes.

What is still not represented, and why:

- **Anything outside the extraction window.** The window covers the demo route
  generously (x −8…18.6, y −2.6…13.6), which is 59 % of the scan's wall band. The
  hallway really runs ~23 m further west and the room complex extends to y ≈ 21.
  Widening it is one flag, not a code change.
- **Oblique surfaces.** The extractor emits axis-aligned lines only. 18.8 % of
  the scan's vertical area is more than 10° off an axis; in-window that is 21.5 m²,
  and most of it is now caught by the clutter pass instead (which does fit
  arbitrary orientations). A genuinely diagonal *wall* would still be missed.
- **The far face of every wall.** Deliberate: the robot can only touch the side
  it walks on, and building both faces makes the slab thickness a fiction.
- **Ceilings, floors and anything below 0.2 m or above 1.9 m.**

## Doors: what the file does and does not contain

The GLB carries **no semantic information whatsoever**. Checked directly: no node
names, no mesh names, no `extras`, no glTF extensions. Its 6 meshes are 6 texture
atlases, nothing more. A door in this file is a patch of triangles indistinguish-
able from wall — there is no object identity to import.

So doors are missing because *the data has no doors in it*, not because they were
seen and skipped. What survives of a door is its geometry: a leaf standing open
is now picked up by the clutter pass as an oriented box, and a jamb as a post
(B08, B09, B16 look exactly like jambs). But nothing marks them as doors, nothing
says which way they swing, and a closed door flush in a wall is simply part of
that wall.

**If door semantics matter, the export is the thing to change.** Apple's RoomPlan
produces a `CapturedRoom` — USDZ or JSON — with labelled `wall`, `door`, `window`,
`opening` and `object` entities, each with dimensions and a transform. That would
drop straight into this pipeline as typed bodies and would remove the guesswork
in the clutter pass entirely. A photogrammetry/LiDAR *mesh* export, which is what
this is, cannot carry that.

## Caveats on the scan map

- The scan's walls are not perfectly straight: the long wall drifts ~0.10 m in y
  over 26 m and the branch corridor's west wall bends from x = 10.40 to 10.26.
  Merging averages that drift into one face per wall, so the built slab is the
  wall's mean line, not its true bow. Worst case is ~0.05 m of model error at the
  ends of the longest runs.
- 43 injected entities → **86 hand contact sensors** on the mjlab path, against 28
  for the tape map. The FSM wall map is rewritten wholesale so the count is not a
  capacity limit, but it is the first thing to watch when this map meets the real
  environment.
- The clutter boxes are *inferred*, not identified. The pipeline knows a cluster
  of surface is there and how big it is; calling one a jamb and another a desk is
  a reading of its size and z extent, not something the file says. Two sit more
  than 0.6 m from any wall: a 1.15 × 0.77 m desk-height object in the end room's
  alcove, and a thin 1.65 m post just south of the bottom wall — outside the
  walkway, so neither affects the route.
- The clutter pass runs on what the wall pass did not claim, so tuning the wall
  thresholds moves surface between the two. That is by design, but it means the
  box list is not a stable inventory of objects.

A small, much denser scan of a ~4.3 × 4.4 m space. Checked against the demo
room's signature before assuming anything: its two dominant walls are at
x = −2.587 and y = −2.252 with **no 1.63 m or 2.41 m corridor**, and the only

wall bumps. It is also too small to hold both curved features, which are 6.76 m
apart. Different space.

## Which means the frame cannot come from the tape

Registering it onto the measured room would have produced a confident, wrong
answer — the ICP would have converged on *something*. So the extractor grew a
`--register self` mode: the frame comes from the scan's own geometry, using the
same philosophy as the tape frame (origin on a real feature, not a bounding box).

- **axes** — the area-weighted dominant direction of the vertical surfaces,
  taken mod 90° so all four wall directions vote together. Here θ = **+0.852°**,
  i.e. the room was already almost square to its own scan.
- **origin** — the corner where the longest x-aligned and longest y-aligned walls
  meet.

```bash
tactile_controller/.venv/bin/python scripts/extract_scan_walls.py \
    --glb "out/demo_map/8_10_2026 2.glb" --register self --name room2 \
    --out docs/demo_maps/room2_scan_walls.json
PYTHONPATH=src python3 scripts/build_scan_map.py --walls docs/demo_maps/room2_scan_walls.json --tag room2
PYTHONPATH=src python3 scripts/check_demo_map_readiness.py --walls docs/demo_maps/room2_scan_walls.json
MUJOCO_GL=egl PYTHONPATH=src python3 scripts/render_demo_map.py --walls docs/demo_maps/room2_scan_walls.json --tag room2
```

## The result

45 extracted faces → **19 walls**, **10 clutter boxes**, 32 free-space
rectangles, **91.8 %** of the in-window wall band claimed by a wall face. MuJoCo
compiles to 30 geoms and every one measures back to the spec within 4.4e-16 m.
The injection path takes it as 19 walls + 10 cabinets → 58 hand contact sensors.
`lane_graph.maps_v3.validate` passes. **Readiness: 9/9, 0 penetrations**, closest

Nothing from the tape map leaks in, and that is asserted:
`tests/demo_map/test_self_framed_scan.py` checks there are **no curved features**
(C1/C2 belong to the measured corridor alone), **no demo anchors**, and that the
start is not the demo spawn.

## No demo route, deliberately

The ten-segment route is a reading of `desired_route.jpg` — it belongs to the
measured corridor. This room has no such drawing, so it gets the honest generic
thing instead: `build_scan_traverse` runs a shortest path over the free grid and
thins it to the corners that actually turn. **4.57 m in 3 segments, minimum
clearance 0.605 m.** Nothing is followed by hand, so no press stance applies, and
the readiness check now says so rather than printing the corridor's hand-switch
note at a room that has no hands to switch.

## Caveats

- **The room is not enclosed.** The scan captured a west wall, a south wall and
  the partitions to the north; the east side has no surface because the scan has
  none there. The built map is open on that side — faithful to the data, but it
  is not a sealed room.
- The 0.99 m deep "protrusion" on the west wall is shelving. It is modelled,
  like all the other clutter, as an oriented box from the unclaimed-surface pass
  — not identified, only measured.
- Start and goal are placed by rule (the longest clear traverse the free space
  allows), because nothing external says where a demo in this room should begin.

# Task-faithful rebuild of the scan converter (2026-08-11)

The GLB → MuJoCo pipeline was reworked to be **task-faithful for tactile
navigation**: geometry that affects walking and touching is preserved
(endpoints, steps, corners, openings, landmark shape); appearance is carried by
a separate look-only layer. The room2 JSON now comes from the new
`8_11_2026.glb` scan (the old `8_10_2026 2.glb` no longer exists).

## What was wrong, measured

`scripts/validate_scan_scene.py` (new) measures any built room against its GLB
in the map frame: boundary distance of every wall-band sample to the nearest
collision surface, surface-normal agreement, doorway widths, landmark surface
pose, sealed-corner gaps, and unsupported ("ghost") built boundary. Run on the
old converter's output it found:

- **Merge-averaging flattened real depth structure.** One weighted-mean plane
  per wall: the new room's west-wall recess (6–11 cm deep, 2.4 m long)
  vanished and dragged the wall face 3.2 cm off its dominant plane; a proud
  radiator strip was averaged into its wall.
- **Corners were not closed.** Runs ended where scan occlusion ended: two
  0.5 m phantom gaps at sealed corners of the upper room — holes a robot
  could walk through.
- **Openings had no model.** Doorways existed only as accidents of run gaps;
  the branch mouth came out 200 mm too wide.
- **Everything not a long straight wall became a min-area box or nothing.**
  The end room's curved seat: a jumble of overlapping "wall" stubs + boxes
  (landmark error 337 mm); a round pillar: a box; a 10 cm floor obstacle and
  an 11k-point structure at the SE corner: dropped entirely.
- **Furniture tall enough to peel as a line became walls** (13 faces in the
  demo room were furniture).

## What changed (same pipeline, five mechanisms)

1. **Steps survive, drift merges** (`scan_map._dedupe` + `refine_runs`):
   parallel overlapping faces > 3 cm apart are structure and stay separate;
   collinear drift within 3 cm merges; every face's extent is then re-derived
   from band occupancy at its own plane (splits where surface is absent
   > 0.35 m); a deeper board within 4.5 cm of a covering front board keeps
   only its uncovered stretches (SHADOW).
2. **Corners snap shut** (`snap_corners`): a wall end within 0.65 m of a
   transverse wall extends to meet it iff the floor mask shows nothing
   walkable behind the face line there — doorways stay open, occlusion gaps
   close. Wall faces must reach 1.45 m (98th pct of their samples) or they are
   demoted to the landmark pass (`WALL_MIN_TOP`).
3. **Openings are first-class** (`detect_openings` → `Opening` objects →
   `MapV3.doors`): gaps between collinear faces with walkable floor on BOTH
   sides, jambs re-measured at 1 cm from tall (z ≥ 1 m) surface only, with
   sub-18 cm occupied runs (a crossing wall's thickness) stepped over; the
   adjacent face endpoints are pulled onto the jambs.
4. **Landmarks keep their shape** (`_classify_cluster`): each unclaimed
   cluster becomes the simplest primitive that preserves footprint and
   reachable surface — cylinder (Kasa circle fit; the round pillar at the
   branch mouth fits r = 0.17 m), boxes along an arc for curved runs (the
   seat: 7 segments; the SE structure: 4), low-poly convex prism
   (`HullObs` → MuJoCo mesh) when a box would misstate the outline > 18 %,
   else oriented box. A cluster whose outline bridges air (edge support
   < 0.80, or p80 point-to-outline > 6 cm) splits along its principal axis
   and each half tries again. Oblique walls that shingled into staircases of
   short axis-aligned faces fold into ONE oriented slab. Small-but-dense
   floor obstacles are kept (a shin-height object is a trip hazard); z-thin
   speckle and sheet-thin far-face echoes on unwalkable ground are dropped.
5. **Visual and collision are separate layers**: `--visual` writes the
   registered scan mesh, cropped to the region and decimated by 4.5 cm vertex
   clustering (no external decimator), and the scene embeds it as geom group 2
   with `contype=0 conaffinity=0`. Collision stays entirely with the clean
   primitives. `scripts/render_scan_scene.py` renders any two scenes from
   identical viewpoints, with and without the layer.

New primitives (`CylObs`, `HullObs`, `Opening` in the scan map) reach the V3
injection path without touching the planner: pillars ride the bulge channel,
hulls fall back to their min-area rectangle on that path (the true outline
lives in the standalone MuJoCo build), openings become doors. Extraction is
seeded — two runs now build the same room.
